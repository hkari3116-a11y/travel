Simbahan

Saint John the Baptist Church

The Saint John the Baptist Church in Calumpit, Bulacan, is one of the oldest churches in the Philippines, founded in 1575 by Augustinian friar Fr. Diego Vivar-Ordoñez. It was built over a period that saw transfers of its initial site and the incorporation of Baroque architectural elements, with the present structure largely completed by 1779. The church has a long history of witnessing key events, from the Spanish and American colonial periods to the Japanese occupation, serving as a site of historical importance, including underground tunnels and a former battlefield

Holy Cross Parish Church (IFI)

HHoly Cross Parish Church of the Iglesia Filipina Independiente (IFI), also known as the Aglipayan Church, in Meyto, Calumpit, Bulacan. It is associated with the devotion to the Holy Cross or Banal na Krus and is located in an area with an important place in the early Christian history of Bulacan. Historical accounts state that Augustinian missionaries reached Meyto in 1572, where a cross was planted and an early Mass was celebrated. Because of this tradition, Meyto has long been associated with the beginnings of Christianity in the province. A shrine was later established in the area to commemorate this history. The church in the photograph features a prominent cross above its façade and a large religious mural depicting Christian imagery, reflecting its dedication to the Holy Cross. It is important to distinguish this church from San Juan Bautista Church in central Calumpit, which is a separate Roman Catholic church.

Kasaysayan

Bagbag Bridge

Bagbag Bridge in Calumpit, Bulacan, has historical significance as the site of a Philippine-American War battle and consists of multiple spans over the Bagbag River. The old, American-era bridge is now a pedestrian park, while a newer bridge carries vehicular traffic on MacArthur Highway.

Matatagpuan sa Brgy. Caniogan, ang bantayog na ito ay nagbibigay-pugay sa isa sa pinakabata at pinakatanyag na heneral ng kasaysayan ng Pilipinas—si Hen. Gregorio del Pilar. Bantayog

Marcelo H. del Pilar

General Gregorio del Pilar in Caniogan, Calumpit, Bulacan. Gregorio del Pilar was one of the youngest generals of the Philippine Revolutionary Army and became known as the “Boy General.” He played an important role during the Philippine Revolution and the Philippine-American War, including the Battle of Calumpit in 1899. He later died at the Battle of Tirad Pass on December 2, 1899, at the age of 24. The monument honors his bravery, leadership, and sacrifice for the Philippines.
